How this is calculated

  • Campaign spending is total disbursements reported to the FEC by every candidate who filed for the seat, including primary losers and incumbents who retired. It can include refunds, loan repayments and transfers, so it runs slightly above money spent on persuading voters. Wesley Bell and Matt Rosendale are left out: their brief Senate bids shared one committee with their House campaigns.
  • Outside spending is independent expenditures reported to the FEC for or against those candidates, by super PACs, party committees and other groups. Issue ads that never name a candidate as an election target, and coordinated party spending, are not included, so true totals are higher.
  • Votes are general-election votes for any candidate, including write-ins and Nevada's “None of these candidates”. Undervotes, overvotes and void ballots are removed. The denominator is the same for every filter, even though primary money also reached primary voters.
  • Periods. 2023–24 keeps every race comparable. The six-year option adds what incumbents spent in 2019–22, much of it fundraising and unrelated to this race. Outside spending does not change with this option.
  • California is one row. Its full-term and special elections shared the same finalists and the same money, so they can't be separated. Nebraska had two unrelated elections and has two rows.
